About

What it is

Resellers earn on thin margins and on speed. The same job repeats many times a day: find a product, check that it is profitable, list it on each marketplace, then keep price and stock in sync. Drill Monkey brings those steps into one workspace for Amazon.com, Amazon.co.uk and other marketplaces.

My role

I owned the product design end to end: problem framing, information architecture, user flows, UI, and the hand-off to engineering.

Business goal

Get new accounts to their first listed item fast (activation), and give active resellers a reason to upgrade their plan (revenue).

One catalogue where every product card answers "is it worth listing?", every item has a visible status, and bulk actions replace repeated forms.

08Key decisions

Three decisions that shaped the product.

Each one answers a problem and a hypothesis above, and names what it cost.

01

Profit before listing

Every product card shows the marketplace price, margin, profit and number of competing sellers. "Is this worth listing?" is answered in the catalogue, without opening the item.

Trade-offDenser cards. The four numbers keep a fixed position on every card so the grid stays scannable.
02

Status as navigation

Listed, Getting listed, Unlisted, Out of stock and Rejected are tabs over the same catalogue. Resellers triage by state every day, so state gets a permanent place, not a hidden filter.

Trade-offThe tab row takes horizontal space, so the price range moved into a compact control beside it.
03

Bulk actions + Automode

Checkboxes on cards allow listing or editing many items at once. An Automode switch per item hands price and stock sync to the system.

Trade-offAutomation needs a visible state, so the switch sits on the card, not in settings.
